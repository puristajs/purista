Use a subscription when work should happen because a fact occurred and the
producer should not wait for or import the consumer.

In this independent example, `Transaction` saves a synthetic transaction in
SQLite and names its successful command response `transaction.recorded.v1`.
`Monitoring` subscribes to that fact and stores one replaceable operational
signal when a debit is at least EUR 100. The banking details are deliberately
small; the subject of the chapter is PURISTA subscription construction,
routing, state ownership, and testing.

```text title="The producer and consumer boundary"
recordTransaction command
  -> SQLite transaction resource
  -> transaction.recorded.v1 success response
  -> EventBridge filters
  -> Monitoring subscription
  -> one derived StateStore signal
```

2. [Add its SQLite resource](/tutorials/subscriptions/setup/add-database/).
3. [Name the successful result](/tutorials/subscriptions/setup/name-result/).

The first three pages form a small command-events baseline for this capability.
They intentionally exclude HTTP, authentication, React, transforms, and the
external provider. You can therefore follow this chapter without rebuilding
unrelated tutorial features.
